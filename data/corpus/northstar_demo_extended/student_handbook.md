# Northstar Institute of Technology — Student Handbook

## §1 Institute Overview

Northstar Institute of Technology (NIT) is a fictional residential technology institute used for the Streaming Live RAG demonstration corpus. The institute operates undergraduate and postgraduate academic programs through schools and departments.

The Student Handbook describes institute-wide student responsibilities. A school, department, residential office, library, or career office may publish additional procedures for activities under its authority.

Where two documents address the same topic, students should consider the applicable academic term, office, and effective date. A later policy does not automatically rewrite historical transactions unless the policy explicitly says that it applies retroactively.

## §2 Attendance Requirements

Students must maintain at least 75% attendance in a course to remain eligible for the regular end-semester examination.

Attendance is calculated from scheduled instructional sessions recorded by the academic department. Students should review attendance records regularly and report recording errors to the course instructor before the attendance-freeze date.

Students below 75% attendance may submit an attendance-shortage request when the reason falls within an approved institutional category. Approval of a shortage request does not automatically guarantee examination eligibility.

An attendance shortage should be reviewed before the examination period. A student should not assume that submitting a request itself restores examination eligibility.

## §3 Attendance Waiver and Documentation

An attendance waiver is an exception process rather than a general reduction of the 75% requirement. Requests must identify the course, relevant dates, reason for the request, and supporting documentation where required.

The approving office may request additional documentation. Approval applies only to the period and course identified in the approved request unless the decision explicitly states otherwise.

Students should retain a copy of the approval decision.

## §4 Examination Eligibility

A student who satisfies the attendance requirement and has completed the required course registration is eligible to appear in the regular end-semester examination.

Students who are not eligible because of attendance shortage must follow the shortage-review process. The Student Handbook does not guarantee that every shortage request will be approved.

Examination eligibility can also depend on course registration status, academic holds, and other requirements communicated by Academic Affairs.

## §5 Examination Rules

Students must carry their institute identification card to examinations. Electronic devices are not permitted unless an examination explicitly authorizes them.

Students should arrive at the examination venue at least 15 minutes before the scheduled start time.

A student who believes an examination rule was applied incorrectly should document the incident and use the academic appeal process rather than attempting to resolve the issue through unauthorized disruption of the examination.

## §6 Academic Conduct

Students are expected to follow institute rules during classes, examinations, laboratories, and residential activities.

Academic misconduct may be referred to the appropriate disciplinary committee. Examples of potentially serious misconduct include unauthorized collaboration, use of prohibited materials during an examination, fabrication of academic records, and submission of work represented as original when it is not.

The handbook describes general responsibilities; disciplinary procedures may be detailed in separate notices.

## §7 Course Registration

Students are responsible for checking their registered courses before the registration deadline.

A student should verify course code, section, credits, prerequisites, and timetable conflicts. Registration shown in the academic portal is the institute's operational record.

A student who discovers an incorrect registration should contact Academic Affairs promptly. Informal attendance in a course does not by itself establish formal registration.

## §8 Student Identity and Records

Students should keep their institute identification information current. Changes to name, contact information, emergency contact, or other official records should be submitted through the designated student-services process.

Students should not share passwords or authentication tokens with other people.

## §9 General Student Support

Academic advising, counseling, residential support, library services, career services, and grievance procedures are handled by different offices.

Students should identify the office responsible for a request before submitting it. A request sent to the wrong office may be redirected and could take longer to process.

## §10 Handbook Interpretation

The handbook provides institute-wide baseline rules. Term-specific notices and office-specific procedures may add details.

When a question depends on a date, academic term, or policy version, the answer should identify that context instead of treating all rules as timeless.
